## 1. Help Hub (Main Help Menu)

### Action Buttons
* **Take Tour Button**: Take the tour again / Start interactive tour
* **Elemental Scores Card**:
  * **Title**: Elemental Scores
  * **Subtitle**: Learn to play common tunes
* **Harmonic Theory Menu Group**:

### Foldable Sections (Help Hub)

#### Section 1: How to Play
* **Title**: How to Play
* **Body**:
  > **Movemental** is a harmonic playground built around an elemental chord diagram:
  >
  > * **Tap chords** to play them immediately, or drag across slices for smooth voice-led transitions.
  > * **Elevator floors** expand and contract chord width from narrow Unison to wide Double Octave.
  > * **Voice borrowing** pulls colorful tones from the opposite vertex on the triangle for dynamic tension.

#### Section 2: Diagram and Clock Face
* **Title**: Diagram and Clock Face
* **Body**:
  > **Earth**, **Wind**, and **Fire** are "parent" vertices at the triangle corners (fully diminished seventh chords in Western music theory).
  >
  > **Child chords** inherit notes from two parents along each axis, divided into four sibling slices (**Base**, **Brother**, **Twin**, **Sister**). Notice how each chord type has a geometry on the clock face diagram, and switching between siblings simply rotates that shape. **Elemental names** are assigned to each chord type based on how much "DNA" it inherits from each of its elemental parents.
  >
  > **Clock Face Diagram**: Displays all twelve pitch classes around a circular dial with parent element colors (**Earth** green, **Wind** blue, **Fire** red). Switch between **Chromatic** and **Circle of Fifths** layouts in Settings.
  >
  > **Four-Level Chord Naming**:
  > * **Elemental Name**: family and sibling slice
  > * **Chord Chemistry**: Earth, Wind, and Fire DNA balance
  > * **Traditional Name**: standard jazz/classical symbol
  > * **Note Names**: exact sounding pitches
  >
  > On mobile, the clock dial and naming readout appear as on-screen overlays; on desktop, they sit in the unified side panel.

#### Section 3: Voice Borrowing Sliders
* **Title**: Voice Borrowing Sliders
* **Body**:
  > Four vertical sliders represent the chord voices (**Root**, **Third**, **Fifth**, **Sixth/Seventh**).
  >
  > The center line plays the pure chord tone; drag up or down to **borrow from the neighbor** (the opposite vertex across the triangle) for extra color.
  >
  > Tap an active slider node again to **mute that voice** (creating smaller voicings such as triads or two-note shells).

#### Section 4: VOICING, IN THE BASS, and Tilt
* **Title**: VOICING, IN THE BASS, and Tilt
* **Body (Tilt Mode)**:
  > **VOICING (Tilt Roll)**: Roll your phone left or right to step through nine elevator floors from narrow **Unison** (full tilt) to wide **Double Octave** (flat), enabling **contrary motion**.
  >
  > **IN THE BASS (Tilt Pitch)**: Tilt forward or backward to shift registers and choose which chord tone anchors the bass, enabling **parallel motion**.
  >
  > With **Tilt to Strum** on, the readout shows your live floor and bass note. With it off, the top value is what sounded last, while the lower value is what you will hear if you tap a chord now.
* **Body (No-Tilt Mode)**:
  > **VOICING**: Select an elevator floor from the VOICING pill to shape chord width from narrow **Unison** to wide **Double Octave** using **oblique motion** (expanding upward from the bass).
  >
  > **IN THE BASS**: Choose which chord tone anchors the bottom voice independently of your voicing floor.
  >
  > Tap the **lock icons** to save your preferred voicing floor and bass inversion per chord.

#### Section 5: Play Style, Settings, and Voice Leading
* **Title**: Play Style, Settings, and Voice Leading
* **Body**:
  > * **Play Style**: **Tap** (Click on desktop) holds notes until you tap again or hit panic. **Tap & Hold** releases notes when you let go.
  > * **Tilt to Strum**: Dynamically re-voices chords as you move between tilt levels without retapping, with tempo rate limits set by **BPM** and **Shortest Note**.
  > * **Retrigger Sounding Notes**: Toggles whether common tones re-attack when changing chords.
  > * **Voice Leading**: Choose between **Root Position**, **Smooth**, and **Smoothest** to control how chord degrees transition across moves.
  > * **Customization**: Adjust **tonal center**, **home octave**, **Borrowing Memory** (Global vs Per-Chord), **Clock Face layouts** (Chromatic or Circle of Fifths), **Glowing Orbs**, **Harmonic Function Labels**, **Synth Presets**, **ADSR envelopes**, EQ profiles, and **audio FX** (Chorus, Delay, Reverb).

#### Section 6: Recording and Panic
* **Title**: Recording and Panic
* **Body**:
  > * **Session Recording**: Captures your performance live in high-fidelity audio (WebM, exportable as M4A/WAV) and standard multi-track **MIDI**.
  > * **Stop & Review**: Stopping a take engages a clean 300 ms fade and opens the review dialog to audition your take, download audio, or export MIDI.
  > * **Panic Switch**: Instantly silences all sounding audio, cuts synth envelopes, and releases active MIDI notes anytime.

## 2. Creation Theory Article

* **Attribution**: Concept by Dr. Barry Harris.
* **YouTube Reference Videos (Editable Titles)**:
  * **Title**: `Succinct demonstration - Chris Parks`
    * URL: https://youtube.com/shorts/NWT86jDvUPQ
  * **Title**: `Succinct demonstration - Thomas Echols`
    * URL: https://www.youtube.com/shorts/OmWSgjwroLM
* **Introductory Lede (Always Open)**:
  > Barry Harris's "Creation Theory" reveals how the entire harmonic universe unfolds from unity into rich chord families. Barry Harris was a Christian, so he compared this theory to The Book of Genesis in The Bible. We paraphrase his poetic teaching metaphors below to honor his life's work and show how Movemental's own analogies derive from them.

### Foldable Topics (Creation Theory)

#### Step 1: 1. The Chromatic Scale (The Universe)
* **Title**: 1. The Chromatic Scale (The Universe)
* **Body**:
  > In the beginning, the Creator made the universe: the **twelve pitch classes** of the **chromatic scale**.
  >
  > This total chromatic collection is the complete world of sound from which every musical note, chord, and progression is drawn.
  >
  > **In Movemental**, the **Clock Face dial** visualizes these twelve chromatic pitch classes around a circular geometry, color-coded by their elemental origins.

#### Step 2: 2. Two Whole Tone Scales (Adam and Eve)
* **Title**: 2. Two Whole Tone Scales (Adam and Eve)
* **Body**:
  > Just as God created Man and Woman (**Adam and Eve**), the twelve-note universe naturally divides into two symmetrical **whole-tone scales** of six notes each:
  >
  > * **Whole Tone 1**: C, D, E, F#, G#, A#
  > * **Whole Tone 2**: Db, Eb, F, G, A, B
  >
  > Because their steps are completely uniform, neither scale has a single home center; they are the **primordial ancestors** of all harmony.
  >
  > **In Movemental**, think of these two whole-tone scales as underlying the geometric symmetry of Movemental. While they aren't represented on the diagram, they give rise to **Earth, Wind, and Fire**.

#### Step 3: 3. Three Diminished 7th Chords (The Offspring)
* **Title**: 3. Three Diminished 7th Chords (The Offspring)
* **Body**:
  > After the fall of Eden, Adam and Eve united to create offspring. By combining three notes from Adam with one note from Eve (or three from Eve and one from Adam), the parents gave birth to the **three fully diminished seventh chords** (four notes each, spaced by minor thirds).
  >
  > These three chords are the first true harmonic generation.
  >
  > **In Movemental**, these three diminished chords are **Earth, Wind, and Fire**, the three corner vertices of the triangle diagram.

#### Step 4: 4. Four Chord Types (Lowering Notes & Siblings)
* **Title**: 4. Four Chord Types (Lowering Notes & Siblings)
* **Body**:
  > From these three primordial diminished parents, generations of children were born by combining notes from two parent pools. By systematically lowering notes by a **half step** (testing all permutations), you discover exactly **four fundamental chord qualities**:
  >
  > * **Major 6th (Lower 2 adjacent notes)**: Contains the exact same notes as a Minor 7th (for example, C6 is C-E-G-A, the same pitch collection as A minor 7).
  > * **Minor 6th (Lower 3 notes)**: Contains the exact same notes as a Minor 7th b5 / Half-Diminished (for example, C minor 6 is C-Eb-G-A, the same pitch collection as A min7b5).
  > * **Dominant 7th (Lower 1 note)**: Lowers a single voice to create a strong pull toward resolution.
  > * **Dominant 7th b5 (Lower 2 tritone notes)**: Lowers two opposite notes across the tritone, producing another dominant color.
  >
  > **Music Theory Note**: While Barry Harris designated Major 6th and Dominant 7th as distinct 3+1 types based on voicing and tonic resolution, in standard pitch-class set theory, a **Dominant 7th** (e.g. C7: C-E-G-Bb) and **Major 6th** (e.g. Eb6: Eb-G-Bb-C) share the exact same pitch collection (inversionally equivalent). Barry loved this duality, showing how a single shape functions as both a tonic and a dominant.
  >
  > For each pair of parents, each chord quality is born **four times** (or twice for Dom 7b5). Barry called these **sibling chords**; in Movemental, we call them **Base, Brother, Twin, and Sister**.
  >
  > **In Movemental**, each child group along a triangle axis (such as **Branch**, **Smoke**, or **Magma**) represents one quality family, split into four **sibling slices** that rotate symmetrically around the clock face.

#### Step 5: 5. Eight-Note Scales of Chords (The Neighbor)
* **Title**: 5. Eight-Note Scales of Chords (The Neighbor)
* **Body**:
  > Every four-note chord combines DNA from two parent diminished chords, which leaves the third diminished chord completely untouched.
  >
  > To build a complete scale for movement and improvisation, Barry Harris combined the four-note chord with the unused diminished chord (4 chord notes + 4 diminished notes = **8-note "scale of chords"**). This allows you to alternate between **tension and resolution** (**on** and **off** chords) and mix them together.
  >
  > By **borrowing** notes from the untouched neighbor diminished chord, all the other types of chords in jazz harmony arise (for example, borrowing one note on a Major 6th yields a Major 7th, two notes yields a Major 7th #5, and so on).
  >
  > **In Movemental**, that untouched third diminished chord is the Neighbor (the opposite vertex across the triangle). Alternating between your child chord (**"on"**) and its neighbor diminished chord (**"off"**) creates beautiful, flowing movements.

## 3. The Elevator System Article

* **Attribution**: Elevator system by Thomas Echols (The Labyrinth of Limitations on YouTube), adapted for guitar from Barry Harris's teaching. The same framework applies to any polyphonic instrument.
* **YouTube Reference Video (Editable Title)**:
  * **Title**: `Episode 16: The Elevator Sequence (Barry Harris Scales of Chords)`
  * URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qYoSZqWLh7E
* **Introductory Lede (Always Open)**:
  > The **Elevator Sequence** is a series of nine voicings that we use for all kinds of **polyphonic motion**: when you have more than one melody happening at the same time, and how they relate to each other.
  >
  > Rather than being purely drop-2 centric, starting from **small floors first** brings our attention to subtle contrary motions and inner voice movements.

### Foldable Topics (The Elevator System)

#### Topic 1: The Nine Floors
* **Title**: The Nine Floors
* **Body**:
  > 1. **Floor 1: Unison**: A single note, or conceived of as two notes that split apart as you travel through the elevator.
  > 2. **Floor 2: Thirds**: Two voices playing thirds, moving along the scale in parallel motion.
  > 3. **Floor 3: Triads**: Three-note closed structures.
  > 4. **Floor 4: Shell Chords**: Guide-tone voicings (roots, thirds, and sevenths or sixths) harmonizing the scale.
  > 5. **Floor 5: Octave Chords**: Essentially a shell chord on top with the tenor voice missing, opening space in the middle.
  > 6. **Floor 6: Drop 2 Chords**: Four-note jazz voicings with the second voice from the top dropped down an octave.
  > 7. **Floor 7: Drop 3 Chords**: Voicings with the third voice from the top dropped an octave, providing wide bass separation.
  > 8. **Floor 8: Drop 2 and 4 Chords**: Wide textures dropping both the second and fourth voices, offering rich flexibility in lower registers.
  > 9. **Floor 9: Double Octave Chords**: The widest voicing, spanning two full octaves between the lowest and highest voices.

#### Topic 2: Relative Motion
* **Title**: Relative Motion
* **Body**:
  > The Elevator is especially useful for **polyphonic lines**: several melodies moving at once. Three classical motion types map cleanly onto the floors:
  >
  > * **Contrary motion**:
  >   * **Mechanic**: Voices move apart from each other and become ever wider, or move inward together. When you play the elevator sequence as a scale, contrary motion is what it naturally does.
  >   * **Application**: Moving between floors widens or narrows voices. Thomas emphasizes not to just go out and in like an accordion or squeeze box, but to change things up by moving between floors and directions.
  >   * **Scale of chords**: Moving one elevator floor while stepping through the on/off scale of chords gives contrary motion by step. Stay on the child chord for the on color (such as C minor 6), then move to its neighbor element for the off color on the next floor (such as an octave chord moving out to a drop 2). The spread widens or narrows while the harmony alternates, so outer voices move in opposite directions by step. Skipping floors of the elevator pushes the voices farther apart or closer together, producing contrary motion in wider intervals.
  >
  > * **Parallel (direct) motion**:
  >   * **Mechanic**: All voices move in the same direction. The spacing between voices stays locked.
  >   * **Application**: Stay on one floor (such as Thirds, Triads, or Drop 2) and travel up or down the scale degrees. The intervals remain parallel throughout.
  >
  > * **Oblique motion**:
  >   * **Mechanic**: One voice stays the same while another moves, or one voice stays static while multiple voices move in different directions.
  >   * **Application**: View oblique motion as a floor plus a floor (such as a unison plus a unison, or a third plus a unison). One line holds a pedal or melody note while another steps through elevator levels.

#### Topic 3: Codifying Movement: Formulas of Movement
* **Title**: Codifying Movement: Formulas of Movement
* **Body**:
  > One of the most powerful things to do with the elevator is to **codify movement into repeatable formulas**.
  >
  > Instead of improvising purely by chance, you can establish an **off-to-on sequence** (such as an octave chord stepping out to a drop 2, or a triad stepping out to a shell) and transpose that exact formula across scale degrees or start it from different floors.

#### Topic 4: Subset and Superset
* **Title**: Subset and Superset
* **Body**:
  > Knowing your **elevator of intervals** lets you spot smaller subsets within a larger chord frame (**superset**).
  >
  > A **Drop 2 chord** contains thirds, fifths, and tenths inside its structure. By recognizing those intervals, you can create movement within inner voices while the overall chord shape remains anchored.

#### Topic 5: Practice Over Theory ("Roll Up Your Sleeves")
* **Title**: Practice Over Theory ("Roll Up Your Sleeves")
* **Body**:
  > Thomas emphasizes that Barry Harris's concepts are meant to be practiced, not merely comprehended:
  >
  > *"This is not an armchair philosopher kind of thing; we have to get in there, roll up our sleeves, and practice if we want to understand anything. Can we do it? That is the only way we can say we really know anything."*

#### Topic 6: Voicing and the Bass Note
* **Title**: Voicing and the Bass Note
* **Body**:
  > In **No-Tilt mode**, changing the voicing on the elevator keeps the bass note the same (**pivot anchor**). Inner and outer voices reshape around that fixed bass, which is effectively **oblique motion**: one line stays put while others move.
  >
  > In **Tilt mode**, roll steps through voicing floors with the **contrary anchor** instead: the bass shifts automatically as the spread changes, so widening or narrowing the voicing produces opening or closing **contrary motion**.

## 4. Applied to Movemental Article

* **Attribution**: Synthesized for Movemental from Dr. Barry Harris's Creation Theory and Thomas Echols's Elevator System.
* **Introductory Lede (Always Open)**:
  > Movemental translates Barry Harris's **Scales of Chords** and Thomas Echols's **Elevator System** into a direct, physical instrument.
  >
  > Here is how each harmonic framework comes alive under your fingers and device motions:

### Foldable Topics (Applied to Movemental)

#### Topic 1: 1. The Elemental Triangle and Lineage
* **Title**: 1. The Elemental Triangle and Lineage
* **Body**:
  > **Earth**, **Wind**, and **Fire** anchor the three corners of the triangle as fully diminished seventh parents.
  >
  > **Child chord groups** sit along each axis between two parents, inheriting notes from both. Each quality is divided into four sibling slices (**Base**, **Brother**, **Twin**, **Sister**).
  >
  > Rotating between sibling slices preserves the chord type while rotating its geometric polygon around the **Clock Face dial**.

#### Topic 2: 2. Borrowing Sliders and the Scale of Chords
* **Title**: 2. Borrowing Sliders and the Scale of Chords
* **Body**:
  > Barry Harris's eight-note scale of chords alternates between your child chord (**"on"**) and the untouched third diminished chord (**"off"**). On the triangle, that untouched diminished chord is the **Neighbor** at the opposite vertex.
  >
  > **In Movemental**, four vertical sliders control **Root**, **Third**, **Fifth**, and **Sixth/Seventh**. The center line plays the pure chord tone. Drag up or down to borrow the nearest higher or lower pitch class from the Neighbor's diminished pool, infusing bebop tension without breaking your chord. Tap an active slider node again to mute that voice.

#### Topic 3: 3. VOICING and Relative Motion (The Elevator)
* **Title**: 3. VOICING and Relative Motion (The Elevator)
* **Body**:
  > Thomas Echols's **nine elevator floors** determine the vertical spread between chord voices. Movemental gives you two expressive ways to ride the elevator:
  >
  > **In Tilt mode**, phone roll steps through the nine floors from narrow **Unison** (full tilt) to wide **Double Octave** (flat), creating **contrary motion**. Phone pitch moves the entire stack to set **IN THE BASS**, creating **parallel motion** across chord inversions.
  >
  > **In No-Tilt mode**, the **VOICING** selector steps through floors while anchoring the bass, creating **oblique motion**. **IN THE BASS** selects inversions independently, and lock buttons save your preferred voicings per chord.

#### Topic 4: 4. Smooth Voice Leading Across the Diagram
* **Title**: 4. Smooth Voice Leading Across the Diagram
* **Body**:
  > Moving between adjacent chord circles on the diagram while staying on the same sibling slice (for example, **Base Branch** to **Base Trunk**) moves only one note by a single **half step**.
  >
  > Movemental's voice leading engine keeps common tones connected and steps minimal as you travel around the triangle.
